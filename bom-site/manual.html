0. Read this first

Directions and names

Put the rover on the floor and stand behind it. Forward is +X, left is +Y, up is +Z. The origin is the centre of the chassis at floor level, so every number in this manual is millimetres from that point. The four corners are FL, FR, RL, RR (front-left, front-right, rear-left, rear-right). At the CNC shield they are wired X = RL, Y = FL, Z = RR, A = FR (as the firmware maps them).

The frame in one paragraph

Two side rails run front-to-back along X with their outer faces at Y = ±195. Two cross rails run left-to-right along Y, butted between the side rails, outer faces at X = ±175. All four rails sit with their undersides at Z = 71 and tops at Z = 91. The deck sits on the rail tops, so the deck top is Z = 96. The motors hang below the side rails on steel L-brackets; the wheels are outboard of the rails at Y = ±215.5 with their axles at Z = 40. Overall width over the wheels is 462 mm, ground clearance 18.9 mm at the motor bodies.

Tools

Hex keys 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4 mm (the kits include them); small pliers; tape measure; calipers if you have them; multimeter (owned); wire strippers for 22 and 14 AWG; a plastic trimmer tool for the driver pots; cable ties (in the kit); blue threadlocker; a printer with PLA and PETG.

LiPo rules for the whole build. Charge at 2.2 A on tile or concrete, never in the rover, never unattended. Meter the pack before and after every run; stop at 11.1 V, never below 10.5. Unplug the T-plug when the rover is parked for more than a few minutes. Mark the two packs A and B. There is no fuse on this rover by your choice: the T-plug on the deck top is the only disconnect, so keep the pack leads short and away from rail edges.

2. Frame

Load every T-nut before any corner closes. The corner kit's nuts are sliding nuts. Once a cross rail is bolted between the side rails, the slot ends are sealed. Miss one and the frame comes apart again.

2.2 Corner brackets (the L-connectors)

  1. Stand the two side rails 350 mm apart, outer faces at Y = ±195. Set the front cross rail between them at the front end so its outer face is flush with the side rails' ends (X = +175). Same at the rear.
  2. A corner bracket is a 17 × 20 mm black aluminium L. It goes in the inside corner of each joint: one leg against the side rail's inner face, one leg against the cross rail's inward-facing face. One bracket per corner, four total.
  3. Through each bracket leg: one M5 × 8 socket cap screw into the M5 T-nut you loaded in that face's slot. Start both screws by hand, pull the joint tight and square with your fingers, then tighten to 3.0 N·m.
  4. Check square: tape measure across both diagonals from outer corner to outer corner. Equal within 1 mm. If not, loosen one corner, pull it, retighten.

Why inside corners: the outside faces of the side rails are where the motor bracket legs hang, and the tops are where the deck goes. Inside is the only face with nothing else on it.

3. Drive

3.1 Motor brackets

The bracket is a 3 mm steel L, 50 wide, with a 51 mm foot that has two 4.3 mm slots and a 51 mm face plate with four M3 holes on a 31 mm square around a Ø22 bore.

  1. Turn the bracket upside down: foot flat against the underside of a side rail, face plate hanging straight down with its outside flush with the rail's outer face. The foot extends inboard under the rail and past it. The bore's centre must be at X = ±125.
  2. Two M4 × 10 hex socket screws down through the foot slots into the two M4 T-nuts in the rail's bottom slot. Each screw lands at the inner end of its slot because the rail slot centre is 10 mm in from the outer face. If a screw will not reach, slide the whole bracket 1 to 2 mm inboard; nothing else cares. Tighten to 2.0 N·m. M5 will not pass these slots.
  3. Repeat for all four: FL and RL under the left rail, FR and RR under the right rail. The four bores should sit on two lines at X = +125 and X = −125.

3.2 Motors

  1. The motor goes inside the corner of the L, below the foot, with its flange against the face plate's inboard side and its shaft pointing outboard through the Ø22 bore. Its leads exit inboard. There is 7 mm between the motor's top and the foot.
  2. Four M3 × 6 socket cap screws from the outboard side through the face plate into the motor's tapped holes, 0.8 N·m. The motor's threads are about 4.5 mm deep; a 6 mm screw through 3 mm of steel engages 3 mm. If the screws that came with the brackets are 8 mm, add a washer under each head or use the M3 kit's 6 mm.
  3. Before the next step, find each motor's coil pairs with the multimeter: the two leads that read about 2 Ω together are one coil. Mark them.

3.3 Couplers and wheels

  1. Slide the 5 mm metal coupler onto the motor shaft with the grub screw over the shaft's flat. Push it as close to the bracket face as it will go without rubbing: every millimetre of overhang adds bearing load. One drop of blue threadlocker on the grub screw, tighten to 1.5 N·m.
  2. Bolt each wheel to its coupler with the kit's hardware. The wheel's inner face should end up 5 mm outboard of the bracket leg, at Y = ±200, giving a 431 mm track.
  3. Handedness: mecanum wheels come as two left-hand and two right-hand. Diagonal pairs match, FL with RR and FR with RL. Seen from above, the rollers touching the floor should form an X pointing at the rover's centre. Decide by test, not by the diagram: in the wheels-up test, drive all four forward; a rover that crabs or spins has two wheels on one axle swapped.

4. Deck

  1. Lay the four tiles on the rail tops, one per quadrant, centres at (±87.5, ±97.5). The half-thickness laps overlap at the two centre seams so the top surface stays flush; tongues face +X and +Y.
  2. Each tile takes 3 × M5 × 8 into the side rail's top-slot T-nuts and 2 × M5 × 8 into the cross rail's, through the counterbores, at 1.5 N·m. Stop the moment the head seats.
  3. Each lap seam takes 3 × M3 × 8 with nuts underneath, 1.0 N·m.
  4. Check that a wire slot sits beside each motor corner and one at the rear centre. Every under-deck wire comes up through these.

5. Under the deck

  1. Battery cradle: four M3 × 12 down through the deck at X ±46, Y ±14 into the cradle's holes, M3 nuts below, 1.0 N·m. The cradle hangs centred under the deck with its bottom at Z = 59, 61 mm off the floor.
  2. Pack: slide in long axis along X with the Deans lead exiting rearward. Retaining bar across the open bottom on two cable ties through its slots. Balance lead tucked beside the pack.
  3. Motor leads: cable mounts on the deck underside, leads routed to the nearest wire slot. The FR motor's run to the shield is about 400 mm; expect to extend with 22 AWG and crimp a 4-pin housing.

7. Turret, post and sensors

7.1 Ceres turret

  1. Turret base plate (100 × 60 × 6) centred at X +110 on the front tiles across the seam. 4 × M3 × 16 through the base's existing holes and the deck, M3 nuts below, 1.0 N·m.
  2. Pan stage free. The Ceres pan servo is continuous-rotation and cannot hold an angle: it plugs into PCA9685 channel 2 and the firmware turns it by timed nudges. Set the turret to the pose you want by hand — nothing moves it at power-up, and every move is relative to where it starts.

8. Electronics on the bench

All of this happens with no motor plugged in.

  1. Jumper caps. With the drivers out, put caps on MS1 and MS3 under each of the four driver sockets. MS2 stays open. That is 1/32 microstepping on a DRV8825 — the as-built v1.0 drive. Put jumpers on D12 and D13 of the A-axis header. Leave EN/GND open. Ten jumpers total.
  2. Drivers. Each DRV8825 goes in with its EN pin over the socket's EN label. Backwards is a dead driver at first power with no warning. Stick the heatsinks on the chips.
  3. No EN pull-up (by choice). While the R4 resets — about a second at boot and at every upload — all four drivers are enabled at full holding current. So the pack is unplugged whenever the R4 is being flashed or reset. (A 10 kΩ from EN to the 5 V header pin would keep them off; the as-built rover does not have one.)
  4. Bulk capacitor. 1000 µF, 25 V, across the shield's VMOT terminal, stripe to GND. Reversed, it vents.
  5. Vref. Connect the pack to VMOT with no motors. Meter from a driver's pot to GND and set it with a plastic trimmer tool: 0.75 V if the sense resistors read R100, 1.50 V if R200. All four. A metal screwdriver slipping onto VMOT kills the driver, and Vref reads nonsense on USB power alone. Disconnect the pack.
  6. Buck. Solder 22 AWG leads to the XL4015's pads. Pack on its input, trim its output to 5.00 V with nothing connected to it (the pot turns counter-clockwise to lower). Disconnect.
  7. Motor cables. Crimp a 4-pin housing per motor so one coil is on 1A/1B and the other on 2A/2B.
  8. Deans lead. One OliRC extension stays whole. Cut the other in the middle; strip both halves 11 mm. The half whose plug mates with the battery is the harness pigtail.

9. Wiring

Every pin, wire colour and length is on the wiring page, drawn from the netlist in wiring.py and cross-checked against it. This section is the order of work and the rules; when the two ever disagree, the wiring page wins.

9.1 How the connectors work

  • Dupont 2.54 mm. Crimped female contacts in a plastic housing that push onto the shield's 4-pin motor headers and the board headers. Rated 2 A; the motors run 1.5 A, so a good crimp is the whole margin. One female per pin — never two on one pin.
  • JST 1.25 mm. The radars' own tiny connectors and pigtails.
  • Deans (T-plug). Polarised, 50 A. Wide flat blade is positive.
  • Screw terminals. Shield VMOT/GND and the PCA9685's V+/GND. Tinned or bare stranded wire, screw down, tug test. Two wires share each shield screw: twist them together before inserting.
  • Solder pads. The XL4015 has four big pads, no terminals: tin them and solder the 22 AWG leads.

9.3 Ground rules

  • One star: the shield's GND screw. Motor return goes there; servo and fan return goes PCA9685 terminal → XL4015 OUT− → the same screw. Neither ever through a board's GND pin or a USB cable.
  • The Pi and its USB devices are one ground island on the bank; the pack side is the other. They meet through the R4's USB cable (the PCA9685's logic ground comes from the shield, on the R4's side). The LD2410Cs return through their adapters and the LD2450 through Pi pin 34 — all on the Pi's side. Do not add another bridge.
  • Tie every run down with the cable mounts. Label the pack side and the bank side with tape.
Power-up order, every time: bank → Pi → R4 over USB, then the pack at the T-plug. Power down in reverse. With the R4 unpowered, VMOT alone drives four motors at full holding current with nothing commanding them.

10. First power-up

  1. Wheels up on a box. Power-up order as above. Command each wheel forward and back in turn, then all four. A wheel that hums but does not turn: check its Vref and the MS1/MS3 caps. A wheel spinning the wrong way: its coil pair is swapped in the housing, not the firmware.
  2. Ten minutes at cruise. A finger on each heatsink and each motor connector. Warm is fine; too hot to hold is a bad crimp or a Vref set too high.
  3. Floor: forward, reverse, strafe, spin, stop. Strafe drift means a mismatched diagonal wheel pair — or wheels sliding at the 1.8 m/s² stops on a smooth floor (drivetrain.txt, traction).
  4. Torque test: wheels up, a lever on one shaft, a hanging scale on the lever, drive at 24 rpm and load it until it skips. Torque is force times arm. The design assumes 0.254 N·m at 1.5 A; a start up a 5° ramp needs about 0.235.
  5. Sensors one at a time: one radar powered, then all three, then a person behind a door. Thermal and camera on a walking person at 4 m. Mic with motors off.
