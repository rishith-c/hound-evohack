VERDICT
  As built (DRV8825, no cooling, 1.5 A) at the design mass 5.29 kg:
  every level-floor case passes — worst F (strafe start, fw a=0.6) at 1.37.
  Below the 1.25 screening margin: C 1.23, G 1.08 — the 5° ramp starts. G governs and is still ≥ 1.0: it should climb, with no reserve for the unmeasured k_dyn.
  G reaches 1.25 at ≤ 4.57 kg at 1.5 A.
  G reaches 1.25 at ≤ 5.19 kg at 1.7 A.
  Most of the mass is ALLOCATED, not weighed: weighing the rover moves this verdict more than any other number.
  Traction: the firmware stops at 1.8 m/s². Rubber-faced rollers (μ 0.5) hold a level stop with
  1.64; hard rollers on a smooth floor (μ 0.3) sit at 0.99 — the wheels slide at
  every stop. That fits the end-of-move veer reported on 2026-09-24; it does not prove it.

  What fails first: a start up a 5° carpet ramp at the firmware's 0.6 m/s². The motors stall
  silently — steppers do not slow down, they stop and lose position with no error.
  Next, in order: weigh the rover; measure μ (tilt a board under it until it slides, μ = tan θ);
  measure pull-out torque at 24 rpm, 1.5 A, on the real battery with a lever arm and the hanging
  scale. Those three numbers retire the allocations, MU_ROLLER and K_DYN.

NOT ANALYSED: fatigue, buckling, impact, vibration, roller wear, uneven floors,
  wheel unloading beyond rigid weight transfer, combined-slip mecanum behaviour (which way a sliding
  stop veers), thermal equilibrium of motors or drivers, battery sag under stall, firmware timing.
  None of this is a substitute for the three measurements above.
