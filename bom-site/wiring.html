Hound — wiring

Seven harness diagrams generated from one net list, rendered with WireViz (open source, Python + Graphviz), and cross-checked by script against the electrical model wiring.py: every load is fed from the rail the model says, every signal link has a wire, the Pi and R4 pins match. The model itself: 0 FAIL · 10 WARN · 5 VERIFY · 14 PROCEDURE · 60 PASS; pack 2.36 A avg / 6.46 A peak, bank 1.48 A avg / 2.41 A peak, Pi USB 0.79 A of 1.2.

The PCA9685 goes on the CNC shield, not the Pi. The shield's comms header breaks out the R4's A4/A5, its 5 V I2C bus, plus 5V and GND: four Dupont wires to the PCA9685's logic header. The XL4015 feeds the board's V+ terminal at 5.0 V; the tilt servo plugs onto channel 0, pan onto channel 2 (the Ceres order), and the fan takes 5 V from channel 15's V+/GND pins, so nothing on the servo side is soldered. The R4 drives the board (5 V PWM to the servos): tilt is positional, t <deg> 65–150 with Ceres's own 600 + 10 µs/° mapping; the Ceres pan servo is continuous-rotation and gets exactly what Ceres gave it, n dir ms = 1250 or 1750 µs for 300 ms then the pulse is cut (about 20° a nudge), and p stops it. The status reports pca = 1 when the board answers. Keep the PCA9685 off the Pi's SDA/SCL: its pull-ups sit at 5 V. One board fewer, no I2C wires between the Pi and the turret, and the thermal array moves onto the Pi's ordinary I2C bus with the ToFs, so the i2c3 overlay and its pull-up question disappear too.
The power scheme, simplified (v3.1). No lever nuts, no resistor boards, one buck. Pack → Deans pull-plug on the deck (that plug is the e-stop) → the CNC shield's VMOT/GND screw terminal with the 1000 µF cap. The same two screws take a second, thinner wire each to the XL4015 (set to 5.0 V), whose output goes into the PCA9685's V+/GND terminal. The tilt servo sits on channel 0; the fan plugs onto channel 15's V+ and GND pins, because every channel header carries the rail. The Pi runs on its 3 A bank and powers the R4, the hub, the webcam and, through the CP2102 adapters' 5V pins, the three radars (four wires each). The ESP32-S3 CSI node has its own small bank. Thermal, ToF ×2, mic and the PCA9685 logic hang on the Pi's 3.3 V header. No pack voltage divider and no alarm: 30-minute timer, then meter the pack.

Build procedures (from wiring.py)

  • cap-polarity — 1000 µF electrolytic across VMOT: stripe = negative, to the GND terminal. Reversed, it vents on the first power-up
  • csi-nodes — CSI v2: ESP32 #2 runs csi_tx OFF the rover (dropped as far along the wall as the room allows, any USB 5 V, channel 6, 100 Hz probes). ESP32 #3 runs csi_rx_udp ON the rover from its OWN small power bank on its USB-C — nothing else plugs into it, no wire touches the rover's electronics. It streams the CSI of every probe to the Pi hotspot (channel 6), UDP port 5005. ESP32 #1 is the spare.
  • driver-orient — DRV8825 into the shield socket with its EN pin over the socket's EN label (the trimpot ends up toward the shield's power terminal on genuine Pololu layouts; clones vary — go by the label). Backwards = dead driver on power-up, no warning
  • fan — The 40 mm 5 V fan plugs onto the PCA9685's channel-15 header: red → the middle (V+) pin, black → the bottom (GND) pin. The XL4015 is set to 5.0 V for exactly this; the tilt servo is fine at 5 V.
  • ground — Servo/fan return: XL4015 OUT−, which is the same node as IN− (a buck shares its negative) and so the shield GND screw — the D11 signal is referenced to it automatically. The LD2410Cs return through their adapters (USB side), the LD2450 through the Pi header's pin 34. Pack current never flows through a board GND pin or a USB cable.
  • i2c-chain — The Pi header has two 3V3 pins and one SDA/SCL pair, but four boards want 3V3 and three want SDA/SCL — one Dupont female per pin. Chain through the boards' second header/STEMMA port (Adafruit-pattern MLX/ToF boards have one), or use a mini breadboard as the 3V3/SDA/SCL splitter. Never force two females onto one pin.
  • microstep — AS BUILT (v1.0 best drive): DRV8825 1/32 = M0 + M2 → caps on MS1 and MS3 under each driver, MS2 open (eight caps), plus the D12 and D13 jumpers on the A header = ten jumpers total. Caps go on before the drivers. 1/16 (one cap, MS3 only, firmware m 16) is the faster alternative — a drive-setting change, ask first.
  • pack-splice — The shield's VMOT/GND screw terminal is the only pack splice: the 14 AWG pack lead and the 22 AWG XL4015 input wire share each screw (2.4 mm² total, inside the 5.08 mm terminal's range). Strip 7 mm, twist the two conductors together before inserting, tug-test both.
  • phases — Motor coil pairs by multimeter: the two leads that show ~2 Ω are one coil. A coil split across the 2B/2A–1A/1B header halves makes a motor buzz and not turn.
  • radar-power — Each LD2410C is a 4-wire job to its own CP2102: VCC → the adapter's +5V pin (never 3V3 — too weak, and the radar needs 5 V), GND → GND, TX → RXD, RX → TXD. They add 160 mA to the Pi's USB budget (checked above). The adapter must be a 3.3 V-logic type: meter its TXD pin at idle — 3.3 V, not 5 V. The LD2450 (100 mA) takes no adapter: 5V → Pi pin 4, GND → pin 34 (pin 6 also works — any Pi GND pin), TX → pin 10, RX → pin 8
  • sequence — Power-up: bank → Pi → R4 (USB) FIRST, then the pack at the T-plug. Power-down and BEFORE every flash/reset of the R4: pull the pack plug first. Reason: DRV8825 nENABLE has a pull-down, so while the R4 is dead or resetting all four drivers sit at full holding current (no pull-up resistor fitted — by choice; the plug order replaces it).
  • servos — PCA9685 on the CNC shield's comms header: shield SDA → PCA SDA, SCL → SCL, 5V → VCC, GND → GND (5 V I2C from the R4). XL4015 OUT+/OUT− → the PCA9685's V+/GND screw terminal. TILT servo on channel 3 (positional MG996R), PAN servo on channel 2 (continuous-rotation); brown toward the board edge = GND row. Fan red/black onto channel 15's V+ and GND pins. Firmware: nothing is driven at boot — the turret starts wherever it was set by hand. Tilt 't <deg>' 78–150 (600 + 10 µs/°), 't -1' = limp; pan only by timed eased nudges 'n -1 300' / 'n 1 300' (this servo turns right below 1500 µs, so PAN_SIGN = −1), 'p' = stop, 'T <µs>' trims the stop pulse (saved on the Pi, set with the remote's hold-still test), 'H <ms>' holds the stop pulse; 'K 1' runs the show about the start pose and 'K 0' returns to it. Status 'pca' = 1 when the board answers.
  • uart — The LD2450 on the Pi's own UART, once: raspi-config nonint do_serial_cons 1 (no login console on it), do_serial_hw 0, add dtoverlay=disable-bt to /boot/firmware/config.txt, systemctl disable hciuart, reboot (pi/enable_gpio_uart.sh). It then reads as /dev/ttyAMA0 at 256000 baud: put that path in ~/rover/radars.json as "ld2450". Nothing on the rover uses Bluetooth. Never land a 5 V wire on pins 8/10
  • vref — Read the sense resistor on one driver: R100 → Vref = 0.75 V for 1.5 A; R200 → 1.50 V. Set all four with the multimeter, motors unplugged, before any motor is connected.

Verify before power (VERIFY / WARN)

  • I-budget — V_SERVO (XL4015 trimmed to 5.0 V → PCA9685 V+ terminal (tilt ch 3, pan ch 2, fan on ch 15's V+/GND pins)): avg 0.75 A of 4.0 A (19%), peak 4.40 A of 5.0 A (88%)
  • V-chain — webcam sees 4.53 V at its 500 mA descriptor (4.57 V at a typical 250 mA) through its own captive 1.5 m cable; USB floor 4.75 V, most cameras run to ~4.4 V. If it drops out at 1080p, run 720p — its cable cannot be shortened
  • V-chain — A 1 m 28 AWG phone cable to the Pi would drop 1.03 V → 3.87 V at the Pi = brownout. The bank cable is a spec: ≤ 0.2 m with 22 AWG or heavier power conductors (a short '3 A fast-charge' cable)
  • connector — Motor phases run 1.5 A through Dupont 2.54 contacts rated 2.0 A — inside rating but the crimp quality is the margin. A contact that heats in the wheels-up test gets re-crimped or soldered.
  • connector — Both servos stalling together = 4.4 A on the XL4015 (5 A peak, 4 A continuous) and 2.1 A per PCA9685 channel header (rated 2.0 A): momentary by nature; keep stalls short with the firmware limits (tilt 78–150°) and release the servos ('t -1', 'p -1') between stations
  • enable — No EN pull-up fitted: during an R4 reset (~1 s at boot, every sketch upload) all four drivers are ENABLED at full holding current. Harmless for a second; NOT harmless if you flash repeatedly with the pack plugged in (drivers heat). So: pack unplugged whenever the USB cable is doing anything but driving.
  • protection — No fuse, no cell alarm, no voltage divider (all declined for simplicity): nothing stops the pack from over-discharging. Rule: a full pack (12.6 V) gives ~45 min of driving — set a 30-minute timer, then meter the pack; stop at 11.1 V, never below 10.5 V. A pack that reads 6–7 V is dead (pack A).
  • usb — Pi 5 only: default 600 mA USB cap → set usb_max_current_enable=1, or use the Pi 4 this file assumes.
  • wire — bank → Pi USB-C cable (SPEC: ≤ 0.2 m, 22 AWG or heavier power conductors): 22 AWG × 0.2 m, peak 2.41 A (ampacity 7 A, USB-C 3.0 A), round-trip drop 51 mV = 1.0%
  • wire — Pi USB-A → webcam (captive cable, 1.5 m): 28 AWG × 1.5 m, peak 0.30 A (ampacity 1.4 A, USB-A 1.5 A), round-trip drop 192 mV = 3.8%
  • bank — Load-test the bank at 2.5 A for 60 s with the meter on the Pi's 5 V pin: below 4.8 V the bank is not a 3 A bank.
  • bank-cable — If the bank's output is USB-C and the Pi 4 is an early board (pre-2020, rev 1.1), an e-marked C-to-C cable will not power it — use an A-to-C cable or a plain C-to-C. Later Pi 4s and all Pi 5s are fine
  • i2c — Before connecting SDA/SCL: power each I2C module alone and meter SDA to GND — it must read ≤ 3.3 V. A module whose pull-ups go to 5 V goes on 3.3 V power or not on the Pi.
  • i2c1 — One I2C bus for everything: the Pi's 1.8 kΩ pull-ups plus the boards' own 10 kΩ. i2cdetect -y 1 must show 0x33 (thermal) and 0x29 (rear ToF); the front ToF appears at 0x30 only after sensing/tof.py re-addresses it (both XSHUT low, FRONT alone → 0x30, then REAR). If the SCD41 is fitted the bus drops to 100 kHz — fine for 4 fps thermal.
  • tilt-servo — The tilt servo must be a 180° (positional) MG996R: with the remote, set a tilt and let go — it must stop dead. A 360° servo keeps turning under a tilt command (it reads the pulse as speed), cannot hold the camera, and winds one way under the show. TowerPro sells both with the same case

Facts checked against sources

  • DRV8825 1/32 step = MODE0 high, MODE1 low, MODE2 high → caps on MS1 + MS3, as built. Current limit = 2 × Vref on Pololu-pattern carriers → 0.75 V = 1.5 A. Motor supply 8.2–45 V. Pololu
  • Raspberry Pi 4: 1.2 A shared across the four USB ports; the docs allot 0.6 A to header loads out of a 3 A supply. Pi Hut, Pi docs
  • CP2102 adapters pass USB 5 V through their 5V pin (≤ 500 mA) and drive TX/RX at 3.3 V; 300 bps–1.5 Mbps covers 256000 baud. Their 3V3 pin is a small regulator output: it stays empty. Kiwi Electronics
  • MG996R-class servos accept a 3.3 V PWM signal from a PCA9685 on a Pi; the signal line carries no power. Adafruit forum, ComponentIndex
  • UNO R4 WiFi: A4/A5 are the primary I2C (Wire), 5 V logic; the Qwiic socket is Wire1 at 3.3 V. Hackster, Arduino docs
  • HLK-LD2410C pins 1–5 = TX, RX, OUT, GND, VCC (5–12 V, 5 V advised), IO 3.3 V, 256000 baud. Hi-Link manual. HLK-LD2450: 5V, GND, TX, RX; its 3.3 V UART goes straight onto the Pi's GPIO 14/15 (PL011 once Bluetooth is moved off it). espboards
