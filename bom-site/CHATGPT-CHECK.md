## The design, in numbers
Frame: +X forward, +Y left, +Z up, origin at chassis centre on the floor, all mm. Frame: two side rails along X with outer faces at Y = ±195; two cross rails along Y butted between them with outer faces at X = ±175. Rails sit at Z 71–91. FOUR corner brackets, one per joint on the two inner faces — a 2020 inner corner has room for exactly one 17 × 20 bracket, and a second on the top faces would sit under the deck. The deck tiles, bolted to all four rails, are the second shear plane. M5 T-nuts at 3.0 N·m maximum or the slot deforms. Measure the four rails first: the longer pair become the side rails, because a cross rail even 1 mm over 350 will not fit between them. Drive: each steel L-bracket goes inverted under a side rail near its end, foot against the rail underside on two M4 T-nuts, leg hanging down flush with the rail's outer face. ## Multimodal detection plan
- Four ways of seeing a person, four different physics: the radar sees RF REFLECTED off a torso (range and periodic motion, through drywall); the ESP32 pair sees RF TRANSMITTED across a space and disturbed by a body (presence, through drywall, no range); the thermal array sees EMITTED heat (a warm blob, line of sight); the camera sees VISIBLE shape (a person-shaped box, line of sight). They fail differently, which is the point — but they also cannot all corroborate the same target: nothing optical sees through the wall.
- So there are TWO TRACKS, never mixed. HIDDEN track (RF only): radar + CSI. VISIBLE track (optical only): camera + thermal, associated by position — a thermal blob and a person box count together only when they overlap in the image. A person the camera does not see is NOT vetoed by the camera; a radiator in this room and radar activity behind the wall are two observations, not one.
- What the RF track can honestly claim: the radar reports periodic motion in the 0.13–0.60 Hz band with a confidence score; that score is a measure of REPETITION, not of life. Tested: a 0.35 Hz sine through the same code reads 21 breaths/min at 7.95/10. A fan, a curtain or a vibrating duct does the same. The label is therefore BREATHING-LIKE PERIODICITY, never ALIVE, and it needs: a still platform (windows during any rover or servo motion are discarded), timestamped samples (the tracker currently assumes 10 Hz), the same rate in two consecutive 30 s windows, and a range that stays put. Radar and CSI agreeing raises it to RF ACTIVITY, CORROBORATED — still not a person.
- The CSI geometry (v2): ESP32 #2 runs csi_tx OFF the rover — dropped as far along the wall as the room allows, on any USB 5 V source — and sends 100 ESP-NOW probes a second on channel 6; ESP32 #3 rides the rover on its own small bank running csi_rx_udp, recording how the room bends every probe and streaming it to the Pi's hotspot as UDP. One bistatic link per stop, and its path crosses the space behind the wall. CSI needs an empty-room calibration for the geometry it is in; without one it reports change, not presence.
- The station protocol: stop; motors off; turret left at its start pose; 5 s radar settle; two 30 s dwells running Walleye's vitals.py while ESP32 #3 streams CSI; then the turret steps the camera and thermal array across five bearings by timed pan nudges (~20° each — timed, not measured, so the bearings are coarse) with the rover still, and comes back; then report both tracks with their labels. About 65 s per station.
- The sentence that survives a hard question: 'radar and WiFi both showed breathing-band periodicity 2 m behind that wall, stable for a minute; nothing optical could see it; go look.' Not 'the rover found a living person.'

## Power and connections
- Pack → Deans pull-plug on the deck (the e-stop: pull it apart and everything on the pack side dies; the Pi keeps running on its bank) → the CNC shield's VMOT/GND screw terminal, 14 AWG, with the 1000 µF 25 V cap across it, stripe to GND. That terminal is the only pack splice: the XL4015's 22 AWG input wires share its two screws, twisted with the pack leads and tug-tested. No fuse, no lever nuts, no resistor boards (v3.1). A fuse and a cell alarm were declined and are listed under Safety as the reviewer's recommendation.
- Power-up ORDER: bank → Pi → R4 first, THEN the pack. Power-down in reverse. With the R4 unpowered the DRV8825 enable pins float low — enabled — so VMOT alone puts full holding current into four motors with nothing to stop it.
- Power bank → Pi USB-C through a cable ≤ 0.2 m with 22 AWG or heavier power conductors (a 1 m phone cable browns the Pi out). The bank must hold 5 V at 3 A: load-test it at 2.5 A before trusting it. Pi USB-A → hub → UNO R4 USB-C for logic power and serial. Fallback if the bank misbehaves: a buck trimmed to 5.10 V on PHYSICAL HEADER PIN 2 (5 V) and pin 6 (GND) — physical pin numbers, not BCM GPIO numbers; pin 4 is the LD2450's supply — and never with the bank connected at the same time; two supplies in parallel fight.
- XL4015 trimmed to 5.0 V → the PCA9685's V+ screw terminal: the tilt servo (channel 3), the pan servo (channel 2) and the 40 mm fan (channel 15's V+/GND pins) all run from it. 5.0 V, not 6, so the 5 V fan can share the rail; the MG996R runs 4.8–7.2 V. 4 A continuous / 5 A peak covers one servo stalling (~2.1 A at 5 V); both stalling at once is momentary. Servo current returns through the PCA9685 terminal to the XL4015's OUT−, never through a USB ground.
- Pi USB (1.2 A shared by all four ports): the webcam on its own port, and a 4-port hub carrying the R4 (radio off) and the two CP2102 adapters that power the LD2410Cs — 0.79 A peak (wiring.py). The LD2450 (≤ 200 mA) takes its 5 V from header pin 4, which sits on the bank's input net, outside the USB budget. ESP32 #3 has its own small bank. On a Pi 5 the default 600 mA USB cap must be lifted in config.txt.
- Radars: each LD2410C is four wires to its own CP2102 — +5V → VCC (never the adapter's 3V3 pin), GND → GND, TXD → radar RX, RXD → radar TX. The LD2450 needs no adapter: 5V → Pi pin 4, GND → pin 34, its TX → pin 10, its RX → pin 8, and pi/enable_gpio_uart.sh moves Bluetooth off that UART once. All three talk 3.3 V UART at 256000 baud and take 5 V power.
- Two VL53L1X share I2C-1 with the MLX90640 only after XSHUT (BCM GPIO 22 and 27) assigns unique addresses at boot. Power every I2C module from the Pi's 3.3 V pins, never 5 V: their pull-ups follow VIN, and a 5 V pull-up on a Pi GPIO damages it. The PCA9685 is NOT on the Pi's bus: it hangs on the CNC shield's 5 V I2C (the R4's A4/A5). If the optional SCD41 is fitted, I2C-1 runs at 100 kHz — that sensor's I2C maximum.
- Single common ground at the shield GND terminal. Never put LiPo voltage on a logic rail.

## Pin map
- R4 / CNC Shield (as the firmware maps them) — X (BL): STEP D2, DIR D5 · Y (FL): STEP D3, DIR D6 · Z (BR): STEP D4, DIR D7 · A (FR): STEP D12, DIR D13 (D12/D13 jumpers on the A header) · ENABLE all four: D8 (LOW = on) · A4/A5 via the shield's SDA/SCL comms header (5 V I2C) → PCA9685 0x40.
- Pi I2C-1 (BCM GPIO 2/3 = header pins 3/5, 400 kHz): MLX90640 0x33, VL53L1X front (re-addressed to 0x30) and rear 0x29 — XSHUT on BCM 22 (pin 15) and 27 (pin 13) — and the optional SCD41 0x62 (caps the bus at 100 kHz). All powered from the Pi's 3.3 V pins 1 and 17, chained through the boards' second headers — one female per Pi pin. The PCA9685 is not on this bus.
- Pi I2S (BCM 18 BCLK, 19 LRCLK, 20 DIN = pins 12/35/38): INMP441 (an i2s-mic overlay). Pi UART (BCM 14/15 = pins 8/10): the LD2450 at 256000 baud, /dev/ttyAMA0 once pi/enable_gpio_uart.sh has run. - Radars: LD2410C LEFT → CP2102 #1 and RIGHT → CP2102 #2 (four wires each, TX/RX crossed, +5V from the adapter), LD2450 → the Pi's UART (pins 4/34/10/8). 3.3 V signalling, 5 V power, nothing metal within 10 mm of the antenna faces. - ESP32-S3 #3 rides the rover on its own small power bank (USB-C only, no wire to the rover's electronics) running csi_rx_udp: it joins the Pi's hotspot on channel 6 and streams the CSI of every probe to the Pi as UDP port 5005.
