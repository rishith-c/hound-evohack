The behind-wall numbers rest on one allocated quantity: how much the LD2410C's gate energy modulates with breathing at range, through a two-sided stud partition. The chart below turns that into a requirement the bench test can check.

The breathing detector needs a modulation-to-noise ratio of about 3

Probability that one window is classed breathing-like with confidence ≥ 5, versus the modulation amplitude in units of the noise floor. Below 1 it cannot see it; from 1.5 it works half the time; from 3 it works four times in five. A 15 s window never qualifies (200-sample minimum), which fixes the dwell at 30 s minimum. Empty-room false alarms in this sweep: 0%.

Acceptance test for the radar height bench session: measured swing of the best gate's static energy over 30 s, divided by its standard deviation in an empty room, at the intended station distance through a real partition. ≥ 3 passes. Between 1.5 and 3, stations move closer or repeat from a second angle. Below 1.5 the through-wall claim is withdrawn for that distance.

A fan is more regular than a person

Breath-to-breath intervals vary 8–15 % in adults; a motor's do not. The classifier calls a window machine-like when its autocorrelation stays above 0.85 at six periods and the interval variation is under 4 %, or the correlation exceeds 0.95.

A fan in the same range gate as a person still wins when it is stronger (the 3 m + fan scenario above): the single-gate model cannot separate them. The real radar has nine gates, and the sequencer keeps the best gate, so a fan at a different distance separates. Untested.

Putting the person on the map: the rover is the rail

Walleye moved one LD2410C along a rail and intersected its range circles. Hound's mecanum base strafes along the wall instead, stopping every 25 cm for a range reading, and its two LD2410Cs are toed out ±30° so the ratio of their energies gives a left/right bearing at every stop. One stop with the toed-out pair already beats a single radar by an order of magnitude. Five stops in 0.2 m gate mode (the LD2410's fine-resolution setting) put the person within about 8 cm, better than the 10–13 cm MIT's WiTrack reached with a purpose-built radio. It costs 20 s of strafing per station.

WiFi on purpose: the Fresnel dither

WiFi breathing detection works or fails depending on where the person sits relative to the link's Fresnel zones, to within centimetres. A receiver that can move 3.1 cm, a quarter wavelength, walks every point in the room a quarter of the way through its zone, so four stops guarantee one in quadrature. Random person 0.8–3.5 m behind the wall, transmitter dropped 1.5 m along it (fresnel.py):

receiverdetection probability
static16%
4-step dither32%
8-step dither35%

Sensitivity falls with zone number, and from one side of a wall the person is always far off the link line (zones 19–83 here), so drop the transmitter as FAR along the wall as the room allows (3 m: zones 19/42/68), keep the receiver on the rover, and dither it in 4 steps of λ/4 = 3.1 cm at every station. CSI stays the corroborating vote; the radar is the one that reaches 3 m.

Mission

4 rooms, 9 stations of 60 s plus 36 m of driving at 0.4 m/s: 10.5 min of a 43 min pack. Repeating every through-wall station from a second angle doubles the station time and still fits.

What the simulation cannot tell you

The radar range law (allocated), the CSI wire format of the RuView nodes, whether a household fan shares the person's gate, camera performance in the actual light, and the MLX90640's real noise on the rover's power. Every one has a line in the runbook. The code that ran here (vitals.py, detectors.py, fusion.py, station.py) is the code that will run on the Pi.
